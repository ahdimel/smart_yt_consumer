## The framework

Spend on the thing that raises your *capacity*, not your lifestyle. Four categories, in order:

**Stamina** — buy what improves the quality of your decisions. Sleep first. Two sub-rules worth
more than the category itself: *buy a habit you won't build yourself* (a gym you'll actually
attend, a trainer who shows up whether or not you want to), and *spend on your specific
bottleneck*, which is personal — his was silence, so noise-cancelling headphones.  `03:03`–`06:05`

**Skills** — favor foundational skills that pay recurring dividends over one-off ones: finance,
communication, negotiation, time management. The operative question, asked monthly: *what skill
gap is currently costing me the most?*  `06:05`–`10:06`

**Security** — hedge three named risks: **concentration** (diversify once you have real savings),
**constraints** (a "BATNA fund" — 6–9 months of runway so you can walk away from a job or a
negotiation), and **complexity** (accountant, planner, estate attorney once finances warrant it).
`10:06`–`14:12`

**Soul** — the category with the worst financial return and, he argues, the highest value: things
that genuinely light you up, the people you love, and generosity.  `14:12`–`18:15`

## The four facts worth keeping

- **The Kahneman $75k ceiling was misread.** His 2023 work with Killingsworth found happiness
  keeps rising with income, but logarithmically — $40k→$80k buys the same increment as
  $80k→$160k, then $160k→$320k.  `00:00`–`02:02`
- **Sleep deprivation degrades reaction time and coordination to drunk-equivalent levels**, and
  worsens the longer you're awake.  `03:03`
- **Archegos (2021)** — a leveraged portfolio that collapsed in days on margin calls, taking
  $5.5bn of Credit Suisse with it. Offered as: wealth is built by concentrated bets and preserved
  by hedges.  `10:06`
- **The Harvard Grant Study** (running since 1938): relationship satisfaction at 50 was the
  strongest predictor of health at 80.  `16:15`
