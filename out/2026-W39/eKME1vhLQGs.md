## The one real idea

Headline inventory numbers understate the problem because not all stored fuel is usable. Tanks and
pipelines need a minimum working volume; only barrels above that floor can be drawn down. So the
question isn't how many barrels are in storage but how many sit above the floor — and on that
measure the US enters peak season with roughly half its normal cushion.

The arithmetic, `03:20`–`07:30`:

- Total distillate stocks Sep 11, 2026: **107.9M bbl**. Three-year September average: **121.3M**.
- At an 85M operating minimum (the 1998 National Petroleum Council "lower operating inventory" for
  US distillate, `04:50`): usable cushion is 22.6M now vs 36.3M normally — **63% of normal**.
- At a 95M minimum: 12.9M vs 26.3M — **49% of normal**.
- Diesel-only ending stocks: 97M vs a 111.3M three-year September average (2022 excluded as a
  crisis year) — 14.3M short. Diesel is ~90% of distillate, which is why the cushion math runs on
  total distillate `05:30`.

Cause `01:01`: refining capacity in the Middle East and Russia is offline from the wars there, so
US refineries run near flat out — 96.8% utilization as of Sep 11 — selling into that gap.
Distillate exports hit 1.61M bbl/d, up from 1.56M the prior week and above average `03:01`. Export
demand is being served ahead of the domestic autumn restock. That is the whole mechanism.

Prices `02:05`: EIA weekly average on-road diesel was $6.29/gal on Sep 14, an all-time high; AAA
showed $6.40 by Sep 17.

Conclusion `08:05`: absent federal restrictions on diesel exports, an October–November shortfall is
hard to avoid. He is explicit that he does not mean dry pumps — he means materially higher prices
and their knock-ons.

## Where the confidence outruns the evidence

The 49% figure — the number the video exists to deliver — depends on a 95M bbl operating minimum he
obtained by asking Google Gemini `07:05`. He says so openly, and his corroboration (total distillate
has not closed below 100M since May 2003) is not unreasonable, but it repeats the flaw in the 1998
estimate it replaces: the lowest level *observed* is not the lowest level *workable*. And the
headline is very sensitive to that guess — 85M gives 63%, 95M gives 49%. An unmeasured quantity is
carrying a 14-point swing.

Absent from the analysis: imports (the Northeast is a structural distillate importer), demand
destruction from $6.40 diesel itself, and any quantification of the harvest-plus-freight demand
increment that the "two weeks" frame rests on. Export restriction is offered as the only lever.

What it does well: every figure is sourced and dated, the obvious objection to his own framing
("97M still sounds like a lot") is raised and answered, and the shortage claim is bounded rather
than sensationalized.

## What the comments contest

Most of the thread is political recrimination rather than domain correction, but several comments
add real content.

- The "no empty gas stations" caveat is the most substantively disputed claim (33): panic buying
  produces tiered chain collapse — first-tier stations run dry, their customers double the drain on
  the next tier, and a region empties. Cited precedent: Hurricane Irma, Florida 2017. Seconded by
  (33) "there's going to be a run on diesel the moment people realize." The video models supply and
  demand but not behavior.
- (17) Diesel and home heating oil are near-identical from a refinery's perspective, so winter tank
  top-offs are distillate demand sitting *inside* his own denominator, unmentioned.
- (229, 38, 30) The SPR has also been drawn down — weekly pumping from ~6M bbl to ~0.4M, effectively
  exhausted. It holds crude, not distillate, so it is not a direct offset, but it removes the usual
  release valve his "unless the government acts" framing implies still exists.
- (24) A truck driver reports two days of work in three weeks and empty truck-stop parking —
  demand destruction already visible, which cuts against the peak-demand collision premise.
- (17) An owner-operator's fuel surcharge nets him $1.50/gal, so the squeeze lands on farmers and
  consumers, not carriers.
- (8) China cut crude purchases sharply after the war began, muting the shock; a restock would
  re-tighten. Not addressed.
- (45) Names the mechanism the video won't: the Strait of Hormuz, closed since March. The video says
  only "wars in these regions."
