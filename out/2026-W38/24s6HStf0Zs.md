Two prepared statements, no questions. Macron speaks `[00:00]`–`[09:09]`, Carney `[09:09]`–`[16:20]`,
and the tape ends before any press exchange despite the "news conference" billing. The occasion is the
first visit by a Canadian prime minister to a French archipelago 25 km off Newfoundland, and both men
use it to argue that France and Canada are literally neighbours. If you watch anything, watch `[07:09]`
(LNG) and `[14:18]` (the taskings) — about two minutes.

**What was actually agreed, sorted by how real it is.**

*Signed on the day:* a contract supplying French space-observation data to the Canadian Armed Forces —
the captions garble the vendor — and an agreement between the Canadian Chamber of Commerce and a French
business counterpart `[07:09]`, restated by Carney as a "strategic partnership" `[15:19]`.

*Tasked, not done:* space agencies and defence ministries to "develop and share common infrastructure,
including launch systems and reception facilities" `[14:18]`; the France–Canada regional commission that
has existed since 1993 to expand into fishing, economic growth, energy transition, climate resilience
and telecom cables `[04:06]`, `[14:18]`. Macron adds underwater cables and aerospace `[05:06]`, and a
bilateral list — defence, rare earths and critical minerals, quantum, AI, maritime tech — that he himself
concedes still needs turning "into real projects, investments and jobs" `[06:07]`.

*Named but unresolved:* Canadian LNG for Europe. Macron frames it as a fuel-price problem driven by
Middle East tension, says bilateral discussions have started and will continue at the G7 `[07:09]`.
Carney agrees to work "short-term immediate" energy challenges and pivots in the same breath to the
medium-term transition `[14:18]`. The distance between those two sentences is the actual negotiation.

*Concrete and small:* linked meteorological services for extreme-weather warning, early-warning
cooperation, and a France–Canada polar research station whose second edition lands on the archipelago
`[06:07]`; easier access to Canadian higher education in fisheries for Saint-Pierre students `[04:06]`.

**The country nobody names.** Macron's "two important independent nations who want to stay independent"
`[02:02]`, Carney's "threats to our sovereignty" `[11:14]`, and his redefinition of sovereignty as secure
access to AI, space, semiconductors, critical minerals and clean energy — all of it points at Washington,
and the United States is never named except as an AI peer. Carney's 1941 passage does the same work
historically: the islanders "rejected collaboration," voted overwhelmingly for Free France, "chose the
right partners," and "history remembers those who act" `[10:14]`, `[15:19]`. That is a story about
choosing between a large neighbour and a distant ally, told 25 km from Canada.

**One claim that doesn't hold.** Macron describes Canada as "an associate member state of the EU" and
attributes the phrase to the Commission president `[02:02]`. No such status exists. What exists is
Canadian participation in the European Political Community — Carney attended the Yerevan summit — plus
a defence and security partnership. Carney notably never repeats the formulation; he says "alliance for
the future" `[12:14]`. Separately, his claim that only four countries are at the vanguard of AI —
France, Canada, China, the US `[13:16]` — is generous to two of them and quietly drops the UK.

**The local grievance underneath.** Macron's fast reference to "a difficult page written here in 1992
that really traumatized Saint-Pierre" `[05:06]` is the 1992 arbitration that cut the archipelago's
maritime zone to a narrow corridor and gutted the fishery. He notes neither leader was in office then.
The fisheries, training and energy language is the offered compensation, thirty-four years later.
