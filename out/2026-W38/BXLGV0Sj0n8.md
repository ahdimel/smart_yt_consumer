## Why magnets, not "rare earths," are the actual chokepoint

Permanent magnets convert electricity to motion with very little waste, and nothing else matches
them. An iPhone has a dozen; an EV motor holds 12 lb; every US missile has at least 12 at the fin
bases for steering. **$0.96 of every dollar in the rare-earth industry ends up in magnets** — so
"rare earths" is the wrong unit of analysis. Magnet rare earths are, and China controls 94%.
`00:00`

Samarium-cobalt came first (1970, two US Air Force engineers), but cobalt prices rose 400% as the
Congo destabilized. In 1983, GM and the future Hitachi Metals unveiled **neodymium-iron-boron** —
smaller, stronger, three times cheaper. That magnet is what shrank the electric world. China was
making exactly zero magnets at the time.  `02:00`–`03:00`

## Rare earths aren't rare. Separation is the moat.

Deposits are everywhere. The difficulty is chemical: **adjacent lanthanides are near-identical in
size, bonding behavior and reactivity**, so no single test separates them. You run hundreds of
passes, each nudging the pair fractionally apart. Rare earths are the hardest commercially mined
family to separate — most steps, most chemicals, most time.  `08:01`–`10:01`

That difficulty is also why the US exited. At Mountain Pass — the richest rare-earth mine ever
found — wastewater was piped 14 miles into the desert. Over 12 years the pipeline **ruptured 60+
times, spilling nearly a million gallons** of thorium-bearing water. Lawsuits and fines
effectively ended US rare-earth processing. China's equivalent mine in Inner Mongolia has the
same waste problem, but rare earths are a state priority, so a suit against it wouldn't be
*lost* — it wouldn't be *heard*.  `10:01`–`12:03`

## The four-step playbook

1. **Build at home** on state-bank loans that don't need to return capital, and party-granted
   land. Sell to everyone; volume is the point.
2. **Shut the door** — export caps and taxes. This hit in late 2010: prices outside China roughly
   quadrupled while staying cheap inside. Two prices for the same material.
3. **Wait.** Foreign manufacturers, now paying 4x for inputs, lose orders to Chinese competitors.
   To get finished magnets they must hand Chinese plants the drawings, specs and recipes. *The
   know-how wasn't stolen; foreign firms paid to transfer it.* An iPhone holds ~$9 of raw
   material and sells for ~120x that — the point of closing the door is to climb the value chain.
4. **Open the door again.** Trade cases take 3–5 years and China usually loses, but by then
   competitors have bled out. Supply floods back, killing the new Western mines the high prices
   had tempted into existence. Survivors merge into two state champions.  `14:04`–`17:05`

## The real chokepoint is samarium

Missile fin actuators hold position by *continuously* running current through a coil, which
generates heat — and heat destroys magnetic strength. Samarium cuts that temperature problem by
~95%. China's monopoly on samarium is not 94% but **99%**; it takes a third more refining steps
and costs 12x more than neodymium. China banned samarium exports outright on **April 4, 2025**.
`18:06`–`20:06`

The framing: monopolies are tolerated while they only extract rent (De Beers, Luxottica, ASML,
Bloomberg). The line is *coercion* — using the monopoly to force behavior. The Pentagon's
response is a price floor of **$110/kg plus a guaranteed volume ("take-or-pay") for ten years**,
with the government taking 30% of any upside above $110 — explicitly designed so China can't
flood the market and wash out US producers again.  `22:06`–`26:10`

## What the comments contest

- **The "organic" claim is the weak point (288 likes).** The video's central distinction is that
  US chip dominance was lost organically while China's rare-earth position was engineered. A
  commenter lists the counter-evidence: antidumping duties, Voluntary Export Restraints, the 1986
  Semiconductor Agreement, Section 301 tariffs, the blocked Fujitsu–Fairchild acquisition, and
  Sematech — decades of US industrial policy against Japan's electronics sector. If that's
  "organic," the word is doing no work. Many other commenters make the sequencing version of this
  point: US export controls ran 2016–2020, so China's 2025 move reads as retaliation rather than
  a sprung trap.
- **The thorium scare is overstated (545 likes).** Thorium's radioactivity is close to background;
  the pollution problem at Mountain Pass was chemical, not radiological. The video leans on
  "radioactive thorium" for its causal turn.
- **Heat damage is worse than described (93 likes).** From someone who builds magnetic NDT
  equipment for 650°F pipe: beyond the Curie point, *each* thermal cycle below it walks the magnet
  through a hysteresis loop and permanently sheds strength. Degradation is cumulative, not just a
  temperature threshold.
- **Attribution (51 likes).** The first rare-earth magnets trace to Strnat and Ray at
  Wright-Patterson AFB and the University of Dayton in the 1960s, earlier than the video implies.
